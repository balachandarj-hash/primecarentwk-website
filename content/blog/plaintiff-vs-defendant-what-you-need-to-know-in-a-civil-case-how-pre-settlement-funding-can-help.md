If you’re involved in a civil case as a result, understanding your role and options is critical—especially if you’re struggling financially while waiting for a resolution.

Let’s break down the basics of a civil case, what it means to be a plaintiff or defendant, and how **PrimeCare Network** can assist with **pre-settlement cash advances** to help cover essential expenses during your legal journey.

### Who Is the Plaintiff?

In a civil case, the **plaintiff** is the person or entity who initiates the lawsuit. If you were injured due to another party’s actions, _you_ are the plaintiff. Filing a lawsuit begins when the plaintiff, usually through an attorney, files a **complaint** and **summons** in court. This legal document outlines:

  * The facts of the case

  * The claims being made

  * The compensation being sought

  * The legal grounds for those claims

### Who Is the Defendant?

The **defendant** is the individual or organization being sued. In civil lawsuits, there can be one or multiple defendants—especially in cases involving businesses, landlords, or corporate entities. For instance, if someone is injured on a commercial property, the complaint might name the business, the property manager, and the owner as defendants.

### Key Differences Between a Plaintiff and a Defendant

The **plaintiff** begins the legal process, while the **defendant** responds to the allegations. Both parties present their side through evidence, but the **burden of proof** lies with the plaintiff. In civil cases, that burden is known as a **preponderance of the evidence** —meaning it’s more likely than not (at least 51%) that the plaintiff’s claims are true.

### Civil Case Resolutions

There are several ways a civil case can be resolved:

  * **Dismissal** : A judge throws out the case, often due to lack of evidence.

  * **Settlement** : The plaintiff and defendant reach an agreement before trial, usually involving compensation without admitting fault.

  * **Judgment** : If the case goes to court, the judge or jury will decide in favor of either the plaintiff or the defendant.
